# Outreach and the opt-out list

POPIA s69 and s11(3)(b); ECTA s45; GDPR art. 21(2)–(3); EU/UK ePrivacy rules (PECR in the UK).
Owner: CEO. Version 0.1, draft. **Counsel to review before the next outreach campaign.**

## 1. The legal point to settle with counsel

POPIA s69(1) prohibits electronic direct marketing (including email) to a data subject unless the
data subject **has consented**, or is an **existing customer** (who bought a similar product or
service and was given the chance to object). A non-customer may be approached **once** to ask for
consent (s69(2)). Under POPIA a "data subject" includes **juristic persons**, so sending to a
business address does not on its own take an email outside s69.

So the earlier view, that personalised emails to organisations' business addresses with a way to
decline were enough, is **not safe**. Whether a personalised partnership or pilot proposal to a
named institution counts as "direct marketing" (promoting goods or services) is a question for
counsel. If it does, the safe course is:

- treat each first email as the **single s69(2) request for consent**: it must identify FORGE,
  state its purpose, and ask whether the recipient wants to hear more, with an easy way to say no;
- do not send follow-ups to people who have not replied positively, unless counsel advises that a
  follow-up is permitted;
- keep a record of each approach and each answer.

For EU recipients, member-state ePrivacy rules on B2B email differ; in the UK, PECR allows
B2B marketing to corporate subscribers on legitimate interest with an opt-out. Counsel to advise
per country before emailing there.

## 2. Rules for every outreach email

1. Identify FORGE (name, address or website) and the sender.
2. Say how the recipient's details were obtained (for example "from your public website").
3. Include a plain way to decline: "Reply 'no thanks' and we will not contact you again."
4. Do not use purchased lists. Prefer generic business addresses (info@, partnerships@) or named
   people whose role makes the approach relevant.
5. Check the opt-out list before sending. Never email anyone on it.
6. Keep a record: who, when, which template, the source of the address.

## 3. The opt-out list

- Kept in [Google Sheet / CRM, to complete] (not in git: it holds personal information).
- Columns: email address, organisation, date of opt-out, channel, note.
- Any reply that says no, unsubscribe, stop, or asks how we got the address puts the address on
  the list **the same day**. Confirm removal in one short reply, and send nothing after that.
- Addresses on the list are kept only to suppress future contact (minimum data: address and date).
- Checked by the sender before every send, and by the CEO monthly against sent mail.
